Poor Man's Magnetohydrodynamics

How a whole plasma can be squeezed into six numbers and still behave like MHD. Learn it in six short steps, then teach it back.

1. The big idea

Full MHD tracks the velocity U(x,t) and magnetic field B(x,t) at every point of space: infinitely many degrees of freedom. Poor man's MHD (PMMHD) asks a sharper question:

What is the minimum mathematical machinery that still keeps the nonlinear MHD interaction alive?

"Poor man's" means extremely reduced, not physically bad. It is not "MHD at lower resolution". Click each step of the chain:

2. Elsässer variables

With the magnetic field in velocity units, \(\mathbf b=\mathbf B/\sqrt{4\pi\rho_0}\), define

$$\mathbf Z^{\pm}=\mathbf U\pm\mathbf b,\qquad \mathbf U=\frac{\mathbf Z^{+}+\mathbf Z^{-}}{2},\quad \mathbf b=\frac{\mathbf Z^{+}-\mathbf Z^{-}}{2}$$

The nonlinear term in the incompressible MHD equations becomes

$$\partial_t\mathbf Z^{\pm}+(\mathbf Z^{\mp}\!\cdot\nabla)\,\mathbf Z^{\pm}=-\nabla P_{\rm tot}+\nu^{\pm}\nabla^2\mathbf Z^{\pm}$$

So \(\mathbf Z^{+}\) is only advected by \(\mathbf Z^{-}\), and vice versa. Try it (one component along a line):

3. Fourier modes and mode coupling

Fourier expansion does not remove space; it writes structure as modes:

$$\mathbf Z^{\pm}(\mathbf x,t)=\sum_{\mathbf k}\mathbf Z^{\pm}_{\mathbf k}(t)\,e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}$$

Take a field with two modes, \(Z=A_1\cos kx+A_2\cos 2kx\). Multiply it by itself, as the nonlinear term does, and watch new modes appear.

Nonlinear interaction is not automatically turbulence. A nonlinear system can be periodic, chaotic or turbulent; turbulence needs many interacting modes.

4. Galerkin truncation: why six variables?

PMMHD keeps only one fixed wavevector. Each Elsässer field has three components:

$$\mathbf X=(Z_1^{+},Z_2^{+},Z_3^{+},Z_1^{-},Z_2^{-},Z_3^{-})\in\mathbb R^{6}$$

Then the PDE becomes a discrete map, and schematically

$$\mathbf X_{n+1}=F(\mathbf X_n):\quad Z^{\pm}_{i,n+1}=\underbrace{\text{linear damping / diffusion}}_{\alpha,\ \beta}+\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_j\Gamma_i^{\,j}\,Z^{\pm}_{i}Z^{\mp}_{j}}_{\text{MHD coupling}}$$
The six numbers are not independent. They are six strongly coupled amplitudes, like six balls joined by springs, not six free balls.

Here \(\alpha\) is the magnetic-diffusive part, \(\beta\) the viscous part (both set by the Reynolds numbers \(R_e,R_m\)), and \(\Gamma\) encodes the coupling geometry. In the ideal limit the model keeps the MHD invariants (energy and cross-helicity).

5. Nonlinearity makes chaos

Set the magnetic field to zero, \(\mathbf b=0\). Then \(Z^+=Z^-=U\) and the model collapses to a logistic-type map (sign convention of Γ aside):

$$u_{n+1}=\beta\,u_n-\Gamma\,u_n^{2}$$

This is a teaching version of that limit, rescaled so Γ is absorbed. Drag β and watch a stable state turn into oscillations, period doubling, then chaos.

<p style="text-align: justify;">
Every year I sit through the same conversation with final-year students. They've finished B.Sc. Physics, sometimes M.Sc. too, and they ask me the same question: "Sir, job ka garnu?"
</p>
Here's the hard part — the subject isn't the problem. The way students plan (or don't plan) their careers is.

<p style="text-align: justify;">
Most physics students in India or Sikkim walk a fixed line: B.Sc. → M.Sc. → try for a college teaching post or a research fellowship. That's it. That's the whole plan. And the math just doesn't work anymore. College lecturer posts are scarce and often ad hoc or contractual (I've lived that reality myself). NET/JRF cracks open a door for maybe a fraction of applicants each cycle. PhD and postdoc seats are limited and take years to convert into something stable. When lakhs of students are all lined up for the same narrow gate, most of them are going to walk away disappointed — not because they're weak students, but because they never looked at any other gate.
<p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
What a lot of graduates don't realize: even GT and PGT teacher posts in schools (state and central, including KVS/DSSSB-type recruitment) are getting saturated now. For years these were treated as the "safe backup" — if research or college teaching didn't work out, at least a school job would. That's no longer true. Vacancies are fewer, competition is heavier, and TET/CTET plus state-level exams are pulling in far more applicants than there are seats. Students who spend their entire M.Sc. assuming a GT/PGT post will always be waiting for them are walking into the same overcrowded gate as everyone else — just one door down from the college teaching rush.
<p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">    
But here's the good news, and it's worth holding onto: a physics degree isn't just a teaching or research ticket. It's a skill set. Math, data handling, programming, logical problem-solving — these are the exact tools other fields are hungry for right now. That skill set translates directly into data analysis, actuarial exams, PSU recruitment (ISRO, DRDO, BARC), banking and defense exams, geophysics, instrumentation, and technical content roles. None of this means abandoning physics. It just means not waiting till the final semester to realize you had options all along.
<p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
